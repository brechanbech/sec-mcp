If Boeing's 2024 debt carried rates 3 percentage points higher, net income would fall by about **$1.56 billion**. Boeing already lost money in 2024, so this would make its net loss about **13.2% bigger**, going from about –$11.8 billion to about –$13.4 billion. If you use the 21% U.S. statutory tax rate instead, the hit is about **$1.27 billion (+10.8%)**.

**Inputs (FY2024 10-K)**
- Total debt at 31 Dec 2024: **$53,625M**. This is the XBRL "LongTermDebt" figure. Its definition includes notes, bonds and commercial paper but leaves out lease obligations, and I assumed it also covers the current portion.
- Interest and debt expense for 2024: **$2,725M**. Against average debt of $52,840M, that is an implied rate of about 5.2%.
- Pre-tax loss: **–$12,210M**
- Income tax benefit: **$381M**, an effective rate of about 3.1%
- Net loss attributable to Boeing: **–$11,817M**

**Calculation**
| | Using effective rate (~3.1%) | Using 21% statutory rate |
|---|---|---|
| Extra pre-tax interest (3% × $53,625M) | $1,609M | $1,609M |
| Less tax shield | –$50M | –$338M |
| **After-tax hit to net income** | **–$1,559M** | **–$1,271M** |
| Pro forma net loss | –$13,376M | –$13,088M |
| **% change in net income** | **–13.2% (loss grows by 13.2%)** | **–10.8%** |

Interest expense itself would rise about 59%, from $2,725M to about $4,334M, and the effective interest rate would go from about 5.2% to about 8.2%.

**Notes**
- I treat the effective-rate case as the main answer. Boeing's 2024 tax benefit was only about 3% of its pre-tax loss, so extra interest would get very little tax relief. The 21% case is the conventional way of doing this calculation.
- The model assumes all debt was repriced for the whole year, uses year-end debt, and has no other knock-on effects. Some interest may be capitalised, which this ignores.
- Because Boeing had a loss, the "percent impact" here is how much larger the loss gets, not a fall in profit.
