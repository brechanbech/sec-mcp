Zillow's filings from the past two years show one large acquisition followed by small add-ons. In 2023 it spent $433M net of cash on acquisitions, mainly Follow Up Boss, and goodwill rose about $443M that year. In 2024 it spent only $7M, with the Aryeo deal in the same filings, and goodwill barely moved. Both deals are tools that real-estate agents use. Total revenue grew 15% in 2024 after two flat years, but I could not get the revenue split by business line, so how the deals line up with the revenue mix is only partly backed by data.

**What the filings show**

| Year | Cash paid for acquisitions (net of cash acquired) | Goodwill at year-end | Total revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | $497M | — | $2,132M |
| 2022 | $4M | $2,374M | $1,958M |
| 2023 | $433M | $2,817M | $1,945M |
| 2024 | $7M | $2,823M | $2,236M |

- **2023, the big deal:** Follow Up Boss is named in the FY2023 and FY2024 10-Ks and in the Q3 2023 earnings release (filed 2023-11-01). It is by far the largest purchase in this period, and the +$443M jump in goodwill matches the $433M spent.
- **2024, small add-ons:** Aryeo is named in the 10-Qs from Q2 2023 through Q3 2024 and in both 10-Ks. Spending of $7M and goodwill up only ~$6M point to small deals that fill gaps around the core business.
- **Earlier context:** 2021's $497M came before the window you asked about. Spending of $4M in 2022 suggests a pause on deals after Zillow wound down its home-flipping business (Zillow Offers) in 2021–22. That is my inference; I did not confirm it in a filing.

**How it fits the revenue mix**
- What the numbers show: revenue was flat at about $1.95B in 2022–2023, the years when the main software deal was done. It then rose 15% to $2.236B in 2024, while acquisition spending fell to almost nothing. That pattern fits growth coming from products Zillow already owns rather than from buying revenue. I could not tie any of the growth to a specific acquisition.
- What I could not confirm: the split between residential (agent advertising), mortgages and rentals is reported only at the segment level, and these tools don't return that. I also couldn't read the filing text, so the deal terms, the acquired companies' business descriptions and management's stated reasons are unverified.

**My interpretation, not taken from the filings**
Follow Up Boss and Aryeo are tools for agents (a customer-management system and listing-media software). Buying them looks like an effort to make agents depend more on Zillow's software and to add subscription-style income on top of its agent-advertising business. Treat this as a hypothesis until it is checked against the segment revenue tables and the acquisition notes in the FY2024 10-K.
