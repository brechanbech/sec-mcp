**Zillow's FCF margin has been stable to slightly rising over the last two years, at about 11–13%. 2022 looks much higher, but it isn't a normal year and shouldn't count as a baseline.**

FCF here = cash from operations (CFO) minus purchases of property and equipment. All figures are in $ millions and come from the FY2024 10-K.

| Fiscal year | CFO | Capex (PP&E) | FCF | Revenue | FCF margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 4,504 | 115 | 4,389 | 1,958 | **~224%** |
| 2023 | 354 | 135 | 219 | 1,945 | **~11.3%** |
| 2024 | 428 | 143 | 285 | 2,236 | **~12.7%** |

**The trend:**
- **2022 is an outlier.** CFO was $4.5B against about $2.0B of revenue. The XBRL tag says reported CFO includes discontinued operations. My guess is that most of the excess came from selling off home inventory as Zillow Offers wound down. The tools didn't give me a split between continuing and discontinued operations, so I can't confirm that or work out a 2022 margin for continuing operations alone.
- **2023 to 2024 is a real improvement.** FCF rose about 30% ($219M to $285M) and the margin went up about 1.5 points (11.3% to 12.7%). Revenue grew about 15% while capex grew only about 6% ($135M to $143M), so more of each revenue dollar turned into cash.
- **If you count purchases of intangible assets as capex too** ($25M, $30M and $28M), the margins are about 223%, 9.7% and 11.5%. The direction stays the same.

Caveats: revenue is the "revenue from contracts with customers" figure. The tools only gave me Zillow's total CFO, not the continuing-operations figure.
