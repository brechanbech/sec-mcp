Paylocity's FY2024 10-K (fiscal year ended 30 June 2024) groups its regulatory risks under "Risks Related to Legal and Regulatory Matters." That section has five risk factors:

**1. Changing laws that apply to its software and services**
- Its payroll and HR products depend on tax, benefit, wage-and-hour, employment and other U.S. and foreign laws. When those laws change, Paylocity may have to change its products.
- New rules could reduce or remove the need for some products, stop new ones from being developed, or hurt how existing ones work and sell. They could also make compliance and new launches cost more or take longer.
- **Money transmitter licensing:** new state or federal money transmitter or money services business laws, or new readings of existing ones, could force it to register or get licenses. Until then, some activities could be limited, as could the way it invests client funds, which could cut its interest income on those funds. If regulators decide it operated as an unlicensed money transmitter, it could face civil and criminal fines, penalties, legal costs and damage to its reputation.

**2. Artificial intelligence and machine learning (AI/ML)**
- Some products use AI/ML. Paylocity has to deal with current and future AI rules as well as social and ethical concerns.
- The U.S., the EU and other places have passed or proposed limits on generative AI and machine learning, which could bring more government scrutiny.
- Failing to handle AI legal risks could lead to lawsuits over intellectual property, privacy, employment or civil rights. Breaking AI rules, or being accused of it, could lead to investigations, fines and reputational harm. Misuse of these tools by others in the industry or by clients could also slow adoption of its products.

**3. Data privacy**
- It handles personal information for its own employees and for clients. Federal, state and foreign privacy laws differ from each other and cover how data is collected, stored, transferred and used, people's rights to access or delete it, use for marketing, and breach notices.
- Three laws are named as the most comprehensive:
  - HIPAA, which applies to its BeneFLEX benefits administration product and its self-insured group health plan
  - The EU's GDPR, with fines of up to 4% of worldwide revenue for the prior year
  - California's CCPA
- More states and countries are passing similar laws, and many existing rules are open to interpretation. An unfavourable interpretation could impose heavy obligations or block some services in some places.
- Enforcement over security incidents and privacy violations is increasing. A finding that it broke the rules could bring large penalties, legal liability and heavy government oversight. Even the appearance of breaking them could hurt its standing with clients, employees and stockholders.

**4. Tax laws**
- New or changed income, sales, use or other taxes could be passed at any time, possibly backdated, and could be aimed solely or mainly at services delivered over the internet. That would raise its costs.
- Each state has its own sales and use tax rules, and their interpretation changes over time. Paylocity could owe back taxes, interest and penalties in states where it currently believes no tax is due.

**5. Litigation**
- It could be sued by clients over commercial disputes, by current or former employees, or over breaches of personal information.
- Lawsuits could be costly and distract management. Insurance might not cover a claim, might not pay enough, or might not stay available on acceptable terms.

**Related regulatory points elsewhere in the 10-K**
- **Bank transfer rules:** under the rules for ACH (the U.S. electronic bank transfer system), money already credited from a client can sometimes be reversed after Paylocity has paid that client's employees and tax authorities. The client's funds could then fall short of what was paid out.
- **Foreign partners:** it uses business partners in countries where it has no operations, which brings regulatory, economic and political risks that differ from those in the U.S.
- **Software errors:** benefits and tax-filing requirements change often, which can cause defects in its software. The resulting data could be incomplete or wrong in ways that tax and other authorities consider significant.
