**Yes, Workday reports a gross retention metric but not a net one.** In its current filings it discloses a **Gross Revenue Retention Rate**. It does not currently report a net retention rate.

**Definition (FY2025 10-K, MD&A, "Gross Revenue Retention Rate"):**
> "Our gross revenue retention rate measures the percentage of recurring revenue retained from existing customers and is calculated by taking total annual recurring revenue ('ARR') of our customers as of the corresponding prior period-end and comparing that to ARR from that same set of customers as of the current period-end. The metric takes into account recurring revenues lost to product or customer churn but does not account for additional revenue earned from add-ons or net expansions, which include volume and price adjustments."

- **What ARR means here:** the annualized value of active subscription contracts at the end of each period. Each contract is annualized by dividing its total contract value by its term.
- **Reported values:** about **98% as of January 31, 2025** (10-K) and **98% as of October 31, 2024** (Q3 FY2025 10-Q).

**Why net retention is missing:**
- In 2024 the SEC staff sent Workday a comment letter. It noted that the company mentioned both gross and net retention rates and asked it to define each, explain how they are calculated, and consider disclosing the actual rates.
- In its September 4, 2024 reply, Workday proposed starting with the Q3 FY2025 10-Q to disclose both. Its draft definition of **net revenue retention** was: "the net change in recurring revenue from existing customers… includ[ing] both positive and negative impacts on revenue, such as add-ons, net expansions, and product and customer churn," calculated as current period-end ARR of retained customers, *including* add-ons and net expansion, divided by total ARR at the end of the prior period.
- The filings that followed (10-Q filed Dec 5, 2024; 10-K filed Mar 11, 2025) disclose only the gross rate. A search of those filings found no net retention metric or definition.
- Several older 10-Qs (FY2024 through Q1 FY2025) also contain the phrase "net retention." I didn't open them, so I can't say whether they gave a figure or just mentioned it in passing.
