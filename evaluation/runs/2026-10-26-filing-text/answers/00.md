US Steel's latest report from before 16 May 2025, the Q1 2025 10-Q, says the Nippon Steel merger was still waiting on US government clearance. The company was fighting the earlier block in court. In the meantime, the deal limited what the company could do: it had to stay in the ordinary course of business and could not buy back shares.

**1. Deal terms and approvals**
- On 18 December 2023, US Steel agreed to be acquired by Nippon Steel. A Nippon Steel subsidiary would merge into US Steel, which would become a wholly owned subsidiary of Nippon Steel North America.
- Shareholders would get **$55.00 per share in cash**.
- Shareholders approved the deal on 12 April 2024, with **98.8%** of shares voted in favour.
- The US antitrust (Hart-Scott-Rodino) waiting period had expired, and all required approvals outside the US had been received.
- The main condition left was clearance by CFIUS, the US committee that reviews foreign investment.

**2. The block and the de novo review**
- On 3 January 2025, President Biden issued an order prohibiting the deal and requiring the parties to abandon it within 30 days.
- On 10 January 2025, CFIUS extended that deadline to 18 June 2025.
- On 6 April 2025, President Trump directed CFIUS to review the deal from scratch and send him a recommendation by 21 May 2025. The 10-Q gives no outcome, since that date is after the filing.

**3. Legal action**
- **Against the government:** On 6 January 2025, US Steel, Nippon Steel and its US purchasing subsidiary sued in the US Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit. They argued Biden's order was political rather than about national security, violated due process and exceeded the President's legal authority. They asked the court to order a new CFIUS review. After the April memorandum, the Justice Department asked to pause the case for 60 days, and the court agreed on 11 April 2025.
- **Against Cleveland-Cliffs:** The same day, the parties sued Cleveland-Cliffs, its CEO Lourenco Goncalves, and United Steelworkers president David McCall in federal court in western Pennsylvania. The suit alleges an anticompetitive and racketeering campaign to stop anyone but Cliffs from buying US Steel. The defendants moved to dismiss some claims; the court heard arguments on 12 March 2025 and had not yet ruled.
- **Shareholder demand letters:** US Steel received eleven letters claiming the proxy statement disclosures were deficient. It says the claims are without merit. It still made extra disclosures voluntarily, without admitting liability, so the letters would not delay the deal.

**4. Effects on the business**
- **Operating limits:** Until the deal closes or ends, the company must operate in the ordinary course of business. It cannot take certain actions outside that without the purchaser's consent.
- **No buybacks:** No shares were repurchased in Q1 2025 or Q1 2024, because the merger agreement bars repurchases without the purchaser's consent. US Steel does not expect to use the remaining **$126 million** of its $500 million buyback authorization.
- **Related-party sales:** Wheeling-Nippon Steel, a wholly owned Nippon Steel subsidiary, is already a US Steel customer, so sales to it are reported as related-party sales.
- **Results driven by other factors:** Management's discussion explains Q1 2025 without reference to the merger. Net sales fell **10%**, from $4,160 million to $3,727 million, which it puts down to lower prices and volumes, lagging spot prices and the ramp-up of the Big River 2 mill. It also discusses tariffs, and expects the March 2025 strengthening of Section 232 steel tariffs to have a "positive material impact."

I used only the Q1 2025 10-Q. The FY2024 10-K (filed 31 January 2025) was identified but not read, so its risk-factor discussion of the merger is not included here. US Steel has filed documents dated after 16 May 2025, but I excluded them under the cut-off.
