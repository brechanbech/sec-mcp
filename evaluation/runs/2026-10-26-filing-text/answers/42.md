**Answer:** In 2024, all of Uber's revenue growth came from higher volume (Gross Bookings). Take rate did not expand. It slipped slightly and took a little away from growth.

Uber doesn't use the term "take rate." It reports "Revenue Margin," which it defines as revenue as a percentage of Gross Bookings. I use that definition below.

| | 2023 | 2024 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross Bookings | $137,865M | $162,773M | +$24,908M (+18%; +21% at constant currency) |
| Revenue | $37,281M | $43,978M | +$6,697M (+18%) |
| Take rate (Revenue ÷ Gross Bookings, my calculation) | 27.04% | 27.02% | about −2 basis points |
| Trips | 9,448M | 11,273M | +19% |

**Splitting the $6,697M revenue increase (my calculation):**
- **Volume:** the extra $24,908M of Gross Bookings at the 2023 take rate of 27.04% adds about **+$6,736M**, or roughly **101%** of the growth.
- **Take rate:** the −0.024 point change applied to 2024 Gross Bookings subtracts about **−$39M**, or roughly **−1%** of the growth.

**Context from the 10-K:**
- Uber says revenue growth was "primarily attributable to an increase in Gross Bookings of 18%," and that this was "primarily driven by an increase in Mobility and Delivery Trip volumes." Trips grew 19%, slightly faster than Gross Bookings, so average Gross Bookings per trip actually dipped, from about $14.59 to $14.44.
- The flat take rate hides an accounting effect. In some countries, Uber changed its business model so that certain sales and marketing costs are now booked as a reduction of revenue. Uber says this cut revenue by $863M in Mobility and $713M in Delivery, $1,576M in total. If you add that back as a rough illustration, the 2024 take rate would be about 28.0%. That suggests the underlying take rate improved, but the reported figure didn't show it. This adjusted view is my own estimate, not a figure Uber reports.
- This split is for the whole company and uses reported dollars. I didn't break it down by segment (Mobility, Delivery, Freight). Exchange-rate effects also lowered reported Gross Bookings growth from 21% to 18%.
