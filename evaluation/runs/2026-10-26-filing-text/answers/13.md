Zillow's FCF margin fell from about 224% in 2022 to about 11% in 2023, then rose to about 13% in 2024. The 2022 figure is distorted, so the real trend is the 2023 to 2024 improvement.

**FCF = cash from operations (CFO) − purchases of property and equipment (capex)**

| Fiscal year | CFO | Capex | FCF | Revenue | FCF margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $4,504M | $115M | $4,389M | $1,958M | ~224% |
| 2023 | $354M | $135M | $219M | $1,945M | ~11.3% |
| 2024 | $428M | $143M | $285M | $2,236M | ~12.7% |

If you also count purchases of intangible assets as capex ($25M, $30M and $28M), margins become about 223%, 9.7% and 11.5%. The trend is the same.

**What the numbers show:**
- **2022 doesn't compare with later years.** The tagged CFO figure covers discontinued operations. That most likely means cash from selling off the homes left over from Zillow Offers, the home-buying business Zillow was closing. Revenue, by contrast, is continuing operations only, which is why FCF came out at more than twice revenue. I tried to find the discontinued-operations cash flow in the 2022 10-K but couldn't, so I can't give an adjusted 2022 margin.
- **2023 to 2024 is the meaningful comparison.** The margin rose about 1.5 points, from roughly 11.3% to 12.7%. Revenue grew about 15% and CFO about 21%, while capex rose only about 6%. FCF grew about 30%, from $219M to $285M.
- **Overall:** Zillow is now an asset-light business with low-double-digit FCF margins that are improving slowly. The large 2022 figure came from closing Zillow Offers and won't recur.

Sources:
- 10-K for FY2024, filed 2025-02-11, accession 0001617640-25-000016 (FY2022–2024 CFO, capex, intangibles and revenue)
- 10-K for FY2023, filed 2024-02-15, accession 0001617640-24-000022 (same figures for 2022 and 2023)
- 10-K for FY2022, filed 2023-02-15, accession 0001617640-23-000010 (2022 figures; the discontinued-operations cash flow wasn't found)
