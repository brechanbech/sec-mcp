The most Snap's convertible notes outstanding at 12/31/2024 could dilute is **about 85.9 million Class A shares (85,945 thousand)**. That's the if-converted share count Snap reports in its FY2024 10-K, in the table of potentially dilutive shares left out of diluted EPS because they would have been anti-dilutive.

I rebuilt the number from the principal outstanding and each note's conversion rate, and it matches:

| Note | Principal at 12/31/24 | Conversion rate (shares per $1,000) | Shares if converted |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 Notes (0.25%) | $36.2M | 46.1233 | ~1.67M |
| 2026 Notes (0.75%) | $249.8M | 43.8481 | ~10.95M |
| 2027 Notes (0%) | $1,150.0M | 11.2042 | ~12.88M |
| 2028 Notes | $1,500.0M | 17.7494 | ~26.62M |
| 2030 Notes | $750.0M | 45.0846 | ~33.81M |
| **Total** | **$3,686.0M** | | **~85.95M** |

Some context on that figure:
- **It's not dilutive right now.** At 12/31/2024 the notes' value if converted was below their principal. Except for the 2025 Notes, which became convertible on 1 Feb 2025, none of the notes could be converted at the holder's option in Q1 2025.
- **Capped calls would reduce it.** Snap bought capped calls meant to offset dilution above each note's conversion price, up to cap prices of $32.12 (2025), $32.58 (2026), $121.02 (2027), $93.90 (2028) and $33.48 (2030) per share.
- **Snap can settle in cash.** It may pay conversions in cash, shares or a mix, so actual share issuance could be lower.
- **The rate could go up in some cases.** It rises if notes are converted after a "make-whole fundamental change" (such as a takeover) or a redemption notice. The 10-K gives no share count for that case, so the 85.9 million assumes today's rates.

**Sources**
- Snap Inc. Form 10-K for fiscal 2024, filed 2025-02-05, accession 0001564408-25-000019. I used Note 7 (Convertible Notes), the net loss per share note, and the MD&A section on liquidity and capital resources.
