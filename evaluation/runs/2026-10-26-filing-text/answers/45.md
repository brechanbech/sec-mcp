Over 2023–2024, Zillow made three small "tuck-in" software and services acquisitions, all in 2023, rather than large transformational deals. Each one supports its core For Sale business. Note 6 of the FY2024 10-K lists no acquisitions closed during 2024.

**The acquisitions (FY2024 10-K, Note 6)**

| Date | Target | What it does | Consideration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jul 31, 2023 | Aryeo | Software for real estate photographers | ~$15M cash plus 380,259 Class C shares worth $20M, so $35M net of cash acquired |
| Sep 11, 2023 | Spruce | Tech-enabled title and escrow platform | ~$19M cash, net of cash acquired |
| Dec 8, 2023 | Follow Up Boss | CRM (customer relationship management) software for agents, teams and brokerages | $399M cash net of cash acquired, plus up to $100M contingent on performance over 3 years. Total purchase price was $484M, of which $402M was goodwill and $86M intangibles |

**The strategy**
- Zillow says Follow Up Boss "is consistent with our strategy to invest in a more integrated software experience for our customers."
- The 10-K names Aryeo and Follow Up Boss as examples under its "Industry Software" pillar: tools that help agents manage leads, create listings and complete transactions. The goal is to increase "the number of customer transactions completed through Zillow."
- Spruce adds title and escrow capability to the transaction itself.
- Follow Up Boss is the main deal. Its contingent-consideration fair value rose by $10M in 2024, and that cost was booked in general and administrative expenses.

**How this fits the revenue mix (FY2024 vs FY2023)**

| Category | 2024 | 2023 | Growth | Share of revenue 2024 / 2023 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Residential | $1,594M | $1,452M | +10% | 71% / 75% |
| Mortgages | $145M | $96M | +51% | 6% / 5% |
| **Total For Sale** | **$1,739M** | **$1,548M** | **+12%** | **78% / 80%** |
| Rentals | $453M | $357M | +27% | 20% / 18% |
| Other | $44M | $40M | +10% | 2% / 2% |
| **Total** | **$2,236M** | **$1,945M** | **+15%** | 100% |

- **Residential, the largest category, is where the deals show up directly.** Residential revenue per visit rose 6% to $0.171. Zillow attributes this mainly to including Follow Up Boss's SaaS revenue, along with better Premier Agent conversion, growth in ShowingTime+ (where Aryeo's tools sit) and new construction. The acquisitions help Zillow earn more per visit from agents through software, alongside its lead-generation advertising.
- **Mortgages grew without acquisitions.** Loan origination volume roughly doubled (+101%) to $3.1B through Zillow Home Loans, which the filing calls "our focus on organic growth." Mortgage advertising revenue fell $12M.
- **Rentals grew without acquisitions.** It was the fastest-growing advertising category at +27%, driven by 15% higher revenue per rentals visitor ($3.65 vs $3.19) and more visitors. The filing ties this to Zillow's own work on its rentals marketplace.

**Bottom line:** The mix is slowly shifting toward Rentals and Mortgages, with Residential down from 75% to 71% of revenue. Zillow's M&A, however, has focused on making the Residential/agent business more integrated and software-driven, while its growth businesses have grown on their own. Strategic intent here comes from the 10-K itself. The filing does not give a separate revenue figure for Aryeo, Spruce or Follow Up Boss.
